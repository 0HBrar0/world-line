# How Steins;Gate Rewrites Its Own History

Steins;Gate runs on one rule that newcomers often miss. Only one version of history is "active" at any moment, a **world line** labelled by a six-decimal **divergence number**. Changing the past moves the whole world onto a different line; it does not create a parallel universe [CONFIRMED] ([wiki.gg: World Line](https://scienceadventure.wiki.gg/wiki/World_Line); [Fandom: Attractor Field](https://steins-gate.fandom.com/wiki/Attractor_Field)). The story has three ways to reach the past, and they behave differently. A text message sent back (a **D-Mail**) rewrites history and moves the world to a new line. A **time leap** sends only a person's memories back, at most 48 hours in the original story. A **physical time machine**, the only way a body travels, nudges the divergence number by a tiny amount [CONFIRMED] ([Fandom: D-Mail](https://steins-gate.fandom.com/wiki/D-Mail); [Fandom: Time Leap Machine](https://steins-gate.fandom.com/wiki/Time_Leap_Machine); [wiki.gg: Time Machine](https://scienceadventure.wiki.gg/wiki/Time_Machine)). World lines group into **attractor fields**. Each field forces the same key outcome no matter how small changes are made, so escaping a fate means pushing divergence across a boundary of about **1%** [CONFIRMED] ([wiki.gg: Attractor Field](https://scienceadventure.wiki.gg/wiki/Attractor_Field); [Wikipedia: episode list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Steins;Gate_episodes)). Only the hero, Okabe, reliably keeps his memories when the line shifts. He calls this ability **Reading Steiner**, and it is why the **Divergence Meter**, the device that displays the number, is only useful to him [CONFIRMED] ([Fandom: Divergence Meter](https://steins-gate.fandom.com/wiki/Divergence_Meter); [wiki.gg: Reading Steiner](https://scienceadventure.wiki.gg/wiki/Reading_Steiner)). Most exact numbers come from two fan wikis that cite visual novel (VN) chapters and anime episodes, not from the scripts themselves. Several popular "facts" are wrong or contested, for example that the story opens on 1.130205% (see the corrections table near the end, which assumes you have finished everything). The report is tiered by spoilers. Stop after the mechanics section to stay spoiler-free; each later tier reveals more of Steins;Gate and finally Steins;Gate 0, the sequel that forks from the original's final arc [CONFIRMED] ([Wikipedia: Steins;Gate 0 episodes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Steins;Gate_0_episodes)).

## Read this first: tags, sources and spoiler tiers

Where the only support is one fan wiki, the tag reads **[CONFIRMED, single wiki]**: probably accurate, but not cross-checked. **[INTERPRETATION]** means a fan or analyst reading, or an inference by this report. **[UNCLEAR]** means the sources conflict, the only source is weak, or nothing could be verified. Disputed points are flagged rather than smoothed over.

The source base has weaknesses. Most mechanical detail comes from the Steins;Gate Fandom wiki and the Science Adventure wiki on wiki.gg. ## Spoiler-free premise: a microwave that texts the past

Steins;Gate began as a visual novel by 5pb. and Nitroplus, first released on Xbox 360 in Japan on **October 15, 2009** [CONFIRMED] ([Wikipedia: VN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steins;Gate_(visual_novel))). Its stated design goal was "99% science, 1% fantasy" [CONFIRMED] ([Wikipedia: Steins;Gate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steins;Gate)). The studio White Fox adapted it as a TV anime that aired from **April 6 to September 14, 2011** [CONFIRMED] ([Wikipedia: VN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steins;Gate_(visual_novel)); [Wikipedia: episode list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Steins;Gate_episodes)).

The story is set in Akihabara in the summer of 2010. Rintaro Okabe is a student who presents himself as the mad scientist "Hououin Kyouma." He runs the Future Gadget Laboratory, a rented room above a CRT television shop, with his childhood friend Mayuri Shiina and the hacker Itaru "Daru" Hashida [CONFIRMED] ([Fandom: Rintaro Okabe](https://steins-gate.fandom.com/wiki/Rintaro_Okabe); [Fandom: Future Gadget Laboratory](https://steins-gate.fandom.com/wiki/Future_Gadget_Laboratory); [Wikipedia: VN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steins;Gate_(visual_novel))). On **July 28, 2010**, at a conference in the Radio Kaikan building, Okabe finds the young neuroscientist Kurisu Makise lying in a pool of blood and texts Daru about it. The world around him seems to lurch, and later Kurisu is alive. His text turns out to have arrived days before he sent it. The lab's microwave-and-phone contraption, the "PhoneWave," sends messages into the past [CONFIRMED] ([Wikipedia: VN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steins;Gate_(visual_novel)); [Wikipedia: episode list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Steins;Gate_episodes)). The fiction borrows from the real world. It draws on the John Titor internet legend, recasts CERN as the sinister "SERN," and turns the IBM 5100 computer into the "IBN 5100" [CONFIRMED] ([Wikipedia: John Titor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Titor); [Wikipedia: Steins;Gate (video game)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steins;Gate_(video_game))).

The follow-up, **Steins;Gate 0**, was released as a VN in Japan on **December 10, 2015** for PS3, PS4 and Vita, after being delayed from November 19 [CONFIRMED] ([Wikipedia (S;G 0 URL)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steins;Gate_0); [wiki.gg: S;G 0 VN](https://scienceadventure.wiki.gg/wiki/Steins;Gate_0_(visual_novel))). White Fox's anime adaptation ran for **23 episodes, April 12 to September 27, 2018**, plus an OVA [CONFIRMED] ([Wikipedia: S;G 0 episodes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Steins;Gate_0_episodes); [wiki.gg: S;G 0 anime](https://scienceadventure.wiki.gg/wiki/Steins;Gate_0_(anime))). Watch or read the original first. A leading fan analysis argues that release order beats the popular "chronological" order [INTERPRETATION] ([Kiri Kiri Basara](https://www.kirikiribasara.com/?p=16724)).

## How the mechanics work: six rules that hold across every version

This section explains the rules the story reveals over time without giving away who does what or how things turn out. The rules are the same in the VN and the anime; the differences are in plot, not physics [INTERPRETATION] ([Fandom: D-Mail](https://steins-gate.fandom.com/wiki/D-Mail); [Fandom: Time Leap Machine](https://steins-gate.fandom.com/wiki/Time_Leap_Machine)).

### World lines are single, swappable histories labelled by a number

A world line is one complete thread of history with its own divergence value [CONFIRMED] ([wiki.gg: World Line](https://scienceadventure.wiki.gg/wiki/World_Line); [Fandom: Attractor Field](https://steins-gate.fandom.com/wiki/Attractor_Field)). Only one is active at a time. The others exist only as possibilities, and the story explicitly says they are not parallel universes [CONFIRMED] ([wiki.gg: World Line](https://scienceadventure.wiki.gg/wiki/World_Line); [wiki.gg: Attractor Field](https://scienceadventure.wiki.gg/wiki/Attractor_Field)). When the world moves to a new line, the old one stops existing [CONFIRMED, single wiki] ([Fandom: Attractor Field](https://steins-gate.fandom.com/wiki/Attractor_Field)). The divergence value is a percentage written to six decimal places, such as 0.571024% [CONFIRMED] ([wiki.gg: Divergence Meter](https://scienceadventure.wiki.gg/wiki/Divergence_Meter)).

What the number measures *against* is never pinned down. One wiki calls it the percentage difference from the "original" line. The other says the meter measures local gravitational variation relative to the line where the first meter was built [UNCLEAR] ([wiki.gg: Attractor Field](https://scienceadventure.wiki.gg/wiki/Attractor_Field); [Fandom: Divergence Meter](https://steins-gate.fandom.com/wiki/Divergence_Meter)). A newcomer should treat the number as an address for a world line, not as a physical measurement [INTERPRETATION].

### Attractor fields lock in outcomes, and about 1% separates them

An attractor field is a bundle of world lines that, despite small differences, all arrive at the same key outcome [CONFIRMED] ([Fandom: Attractor Field](https://steins-gate.fandom.com/wiki/Attractor_Field); [wiki.gg: Attractor Field](https://scienceadventure.wiki.gg/wiki/Attractor_Field)). This is called **convergence**. Small changes that keep the same result do nothing: the world works to bring about the fixed event anyway. Only a change big enough to leave the field, a "major divergence," escapes it [CONFIRMED, single wiki citing the VN] ([wiki.gg: Attractor Field](https://scienceadventure.wiki.gg/wiki/Attractor_Field)). The two wikis frame this force differently. Fandom says some outcomes are simply more probable and so appear on more lines. wiki.gg describes the world actively preventing change [UNCLEAR] ([Fandom: Attractor Field](https://steins-gate.fandom.com/wiki/Attractor_Field); [wiki.gg: Attractor Field](https://scienceadventure.wiki.gg/wiki/Attractor_Field)).

The two fields that matter are **Alpha** (values starting 0.x%) and **Beta** (values starting 1.x%) [CONFIRMED] ([Fandom: Attractor Field](https://steins-gate.fandom.com/wiki/Attractor_Field)). wiki.gg warns that reading the field from the first digit is only a rule of thumb and lists exceptions [CONFIRMED] ([wiki.gg: World Line](https://scienceadventure.wiki.gg/wiki/World_Line)). The anime refers explicitly to crossing a **1% divergence** point, in episodes 7 and 14 [CONFIRMED] ([Wikipedia: episode list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Steins;Gate_episodes)). A physicist's blog says Okabe must move more than 4% away. Every other source points to 1%, so the 4% figure is almost certainly an error [UNCLEAR] ([Galileo Unbound](https://galileo-unbound.blog/tag/steins-gate/)). No reference source describes a "2% barrier." Lines above 2% (labelled Gamma, Delta and so on) appear only in spin-offs [CONFIRMED] ([wiki.gg: World Line](https://scienceadventure.wiki.gg/wiki/World_Line); [Fandom: Attractor Field](https://steins-gate.fandom.com/wiki/Attractor_Field)). The same physicist compares attractor fields to "basins of attraction" in dynamical systems: different starting conditions get pulled back to the same result, the opposite of the butterfly effect [INTERPRETATION] ([Galileo Unbound](https://galileo-unbound.blog/tag/steins-gate/)).

### The Divergence Meter shows the number, but only Okabe can use it

The Divergence Meter is an array of Nixie tubes that shows the current line's value to six decimal places [CONFIRMED] ([Fandom: Divergence Meter](https://steins-gate.fandom.com/wiki/Divergence_Meter); [wiki.gg: Divergence Meter](https://scienceadventure.wiki.gg/wiki/Divergence_Meter)). Changes smaller than the sixth decimal do not register, and the display cannot show negative values [CONFIRMED, single wiki] ([Fandom: Divergence Meter](https://steins-gate.fandom.com/wiki/Divergence_Meter)). One wiki quotes series creator Chiyomaru Shikura describing the meter as a seven-digit display of world-line fluctuation, with changes far past the decimal point amounting to no noticeable difference [CONFIRMED, single wiki citing a creator interview] ([wiki.gg: Divergence Meter](https://scienceadventure.wiki.gg/wiki/Divergence_Meter)).

This is where the meter and the attractor fields connect. Okabe watches for the leading digit to change, for example from 0.x to 1.x, to know he has crossed into another attractor field. Paired with his Reading Steiner, the reading tells him which line and which field he is in [CONFIRMED] ([Fandom: Divergence Meter](https://steins-gate.fandom.com/wiki/Divergence_Meter); [wiki.gg: Divergence Meter](https://scienceadventure.wiki.gg/wiki/Divergence_Meter)). Only Okabe can use it meaningfully. A shift rewrites everyone else's memories, including their memory of the previous reading, so to them the number never seems to change [CONFIRMED] ([Fandom: Divergence Meter](https://steins-gate.fandom.com/wiki/Divergence_Meter); [wiki.gg: Divergence Meter](https://scienceadventure.wiki.gg/wiki/Divergence_Meter)). Where the meter comes from is a Tier 2 spoiler.

### How D-Mails change world lines

The PhoneWave (name subject to change) is a microwave oven wired to a mobile phone. It works only while a large CRT television in the shop downstairs is switched on, acting as a "lifter" [CONFIRMED] ([wiki.gg: PhoneWave](https://scienceadventure.wiki.gg/wiki/PhoneWave); [Wikipedia: episode list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Steins;Gate_episodes)). The fiction's explanation runs as follows. The microwave's waves interacting with the phone signal accelerate particles into tiny rotating (Kerr) black holes. Under the right conditions their event horizon disappears, leaving a passable "naked singularity," and the CRT supplies the electrons that make this happen [CONFIRMED] ([wiki.gg: PhoneWave](https://scienceadventure.wiki.gg/wiki/PhoneWave); [Fandom: D-Mail](https://steins-gate.fandom.com/wiki/D-Mail); [LP Archive, VN scene](https://lparchive.org/SteinsGate/Update%2079)). Data passes through. Physical objects come back as gel, which is how the lab discovers the effect, with jellied bananas [CONFIRMED] ([Wikipedia: episode list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Steins;Gate_episodes); [wiki.gg: PhoneWave](https://scienceadventure.wiki.gg/wiki/PhoneWave)).

A **D-Mail** ("DeLorean Mail") is a text sent into the past through the PhoneWave [CONFIRMED] ([Wikipedia: Steins;Gate (video game)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steins;Gate_(video_game)); [Fandom: D-Mail](https://steins-gate.fandom.com/wiki/D-Mail)). It holds about **36 bytes**, split into three 12-byte segments. That is roughly 36 English or 18 Japanese characters, and anything longer is lost [CONFIRMED] ([Fandom: D-Mail](https://steins-gate.fandom.com/wiki/D-Mail); [wiki.gg: PhoneWave](https://scienceadventure.wiki.gg/wiki/PhoneWave)). The microwave's timer sets how far back it goes, in one-hour steps, but no source gives the conversion formula [CONFIRMED for hour steps; UNCLEAR for the formula] ([wiki.gg: PhoneWave](https://scienceadventure.wiki.gg/wiki/PhoneWave); [Fandom: D-Mail](https://steins-gate.fandom.com/wiki/D-Mail)). No hard maximum range is stated. D-Mails in the story reach as far back as 1993 and 2000 [CONFIRMED that such sends occur; UNCLEAR on any limit] ([Fandom: D-Mail](https://steins-gate.fandom.com/wiki/D-Mail)).

A D-Mail changes the world line when its arrival changes something in the past. Fandom's model works like this. The world becomes the most similar line in which the mail's effects have played out. The "present" stays at the same moment, but history up to it is now different. Okabe feels the shift at the moment of sending, when the line is rebuilt, not when the mail is received [CONFIRMED, single wiki; the wiki's own synthesis, so close to INTERPRETATION] ([Fandom: D-Mail](https://steins-gate.fandom.com/wiki/D-Mail)). The physical world changes along with everyone's memories. Places, possessions and even bodies can be different afterwards [CONFIRMED] ([Fandom: Reading Steiner](https://steins-gate.fandom.com/wiki/Reading_Steiner); [IMDb plot summary](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt2156197/plotsummary)).

### How time leaps work: memories travel, bodies stay

The **Time Leap Machine** is an upgrade to the PhoneWave, introduced at the series' midpoint (anime episodes 11–12). It sends a person's memories, not their body, up to two days into the past [CONFIRMED] ([Wikipedia: episode list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Steins;Gate_episodes); [Fandom: Time Leap Machine](https://steins-gate.fandom.com/wiki/Time_Leap_Machine)). Both wikis agree on how it works. A headset reads the brain's memory signals and turns them into data. The data is squeezed down to D-Mail size using a black hole from SERN's particle accelerator, sent through the PhoneWave to the leaper's own phone in the past, and delivered through the earpiece into the past self's brain [CONFIRMED] ([wiki.gg: Time Leap Machine](https://scienceadventure.wiki.gg/wiki/Time_Leap_Machine); [Fandom: Time Leap Machine](https://steins-gate.fandom.com/wiki/Time_Leap_Machine)). One wiki adds exact figures: about 3.24 TB of memory data, sent over 64 lines in about 45 seconds and compressed to 36 bytes in about 23 milliseconds [CONFIRMED, single wiki] ([wiki.gg: Time Leap Machine](https://scienceadventure.wiki.gg/wiki/Time_Leap_Machine)).

What travels is **memory only**. The body stays in its own time, and one wiki specifies that consciousness and personality are not transmitted as such [CONFIRMED that only memories travel; the consciousness detail is single wiki] ([wiki.gg: Time Leap Machine](https://scienceadventure.wiki.gg/wiki/Time_Leap_Machine); [Wikipedia: Steins;Gate (video game)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steins;Gate_(video_game))). The past self's phone must be on and with them, and they must answer it and hold it to their ear; if they don't, the leap fails [CONFIRMED] ([Fandom: Time Leap Machine](https://steins-gate.fandom.com/wiki/Time_Leap_Machine); [wiki.gg: Time Leap Machine](https://scienceadventure.wiki.gg/wiki/Time_Leap_Machine)). The target can only be your own brain, because every brain is different [CONFIRMED, single wiki] ([Fandom: Time Leap Machine](https://steins-gate.fandom.com/wiki/Time_Leap_Machine)). **48 hours** is the safe maximum. Fandom explains that the brain changes over time, so memories sent further back may not fit, risking brain damage or brain death [CONFIRMED for 48 hours; the reason is single wiki] ([Fandom: Time Leap Machine](https://steins-gate.fandom.com/wiki/Time_Leap_Machine); [wiki.gg: Time Leap Machine](https://scienceadventure.wiki.gg/wiki/Time_Leap_Machine)). An upgrade in the sequel extends the safe range to **two weeks** [CONFIRMED] ([Fandom: Time Leap Machine](https://steins-gate.fandom.com/wiki/Time_Leap_Machine); [wiki.gg: Time Leap Machine](https://scienceadventure.wiki.gg/wiki/Time_Leap_Machine)).

The important contrast with D-Mails: **a leap by itself does not trigger Reading Steiner**, including when Okabe returns to the moment he leaped from [CONFIRMED, single wiki citing the VN] ([Fandom: Reading Steiner](https://steins-gate.fandom.com/wiki/Reading_Steiner)). No source says a leap on its own shifts the divergence. Fandom says only that leaps *combined with* D-Mails let someone move between lines [UNCLEAR] ([Fandom: Time Leap Machine](https://steins-gate.fandom.com/wiki/Time_Leap_Machine)). The best-supported reading is this: a leap moves the leaper's "present" back within the same world line, and the line changes only if the leaper then does something big enough to change the past [INTERPRETATION] ([votuko PDF](https://gwern.net/doc/fiction/science-fiction/time-travel/2023-votuko-themechanicsofsteinsgate.pdf); [Fandom: Reading Steiner](https://steins-gate.fandom.com/wiki/Reading_Steiner)).

### How Reading Steiner works

Reading Steiner is Okabe's own name for his ability to keep his memories when the world line changes. Everyone else's memories are rewritten to fit the new line. In the anime he names and explains it in episode 8 [CONFIRMED] ([Fandom: Reading Steiner](https://steins-gate.fandom.com/wiki/Reading_Steiner); [wiki.gg: Reading Steiner](https://scienceadventure.wiki.gg/wiki/Reading_Steiner); [Wikipedia: episode list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Steins;Gate_episodes)). Mechanically, when a line is rebuilt, his memories from the old line are copied over and replace the new line's version of him, from the moment the D-Mail took effect [CONFIRMED] ([Fandom: Reading Steiner](https://steins-gate.fandom.com/wiki/Reading_Steiner); [wiki.gg: Reading Steiner](https://scienceadventure.wiki.gg/wiki/Reading_Steiner)). It triggers only when a D-Mail, or some action in the present, changes the divergence enough to rebuild the line. He feels brief vertigo, and for a few seconds the world turns grey, blurry and twisted [CONFIRMED] ([Fandom: Reading Steiner](https://steins-gate.fandom.com/wiki/Reading_Steiner); [wiki.gg: Reading Steiner](https://scienceadventure.wiki.gg/wiki/Reading_Steiner)).

It does not trigger in three cases: a change too small to move the meter, a time leap, and physical time travellers [CONFIRMED, single wiki] ([Fandom: Reading Steiner](https://steins-gate.fandom.com/wiki/Reading_Steiner)). Physical travellers are not rebuilt with the line at all, so they keep their memories anyway [CONFIRMED, single wiki] ([Fandom: Reading Steiner](https://steins-gate.fandom.com/wiki/Reading_Steiner)). Where the ability comes from is never explained. A high fever when Okabe was about eight, around Y2K, is implied, and anime episode 10 hints at a childhood origin [CONFIRMED that it is implied; the cause is UNCLEAR] ([wiki.gg: Reading Steiner](https://scienceadventure.wiki.gg/wiki/Reading_Steiner); [Wikipedia: VN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steins;Gate_(visual_novel)); [Wikipedia: episode list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Steins;Gate_episodes)). Okabe is not the only one who remembers. Other characters catch weaker echoes as déjà vu or dreams, and Okabe himself wonders whether everyone has the ability to some degree [CONFIRMED] ([Fandom: Reading Steiner](https://steins-gate.fandom.com/wiki/Reading_Steiner)). The examples are spoilers and appear in the tiers below.

## SPOILERS: Steins;Gate first half — one text message flips the world from Beta to Alpha

*Covers anime episodes 1–12 and the VN up to the raid on the lab.*

### The story opens on Beta 1.130426%, not 1.130205%

The first line of the story is a **Beta** line. The value typed and displayed in the VN prologue is **1.130426%** [CONFIRMED] ([wiki.gg: Beta Attractor Field](https://scienceadventure.wiki.gg/wiki/Beta_Attractor_Field); [Fandom: D-Mail](https://steins-gate.fandom.com/wiki/D-Mail)). wiki.gg also records **1.130246%** as the "real" value in the VN script, a transposed-digit discrepancy that could not be checked against the text itself. Fandom's timeline instead labels the July 28 Beta state 1.129848%, a value otherwise tied to Steins;Gate 0 [UNCLEAR; 1.130426% is best supported] ([wiki.gg: Beta Attractor Field](https://scienceadventure.wiki.gg/wiki/Beta_Attractor_Field); [Fandom: Timeline](https://steins-gate.fandom.com/wiki/Timeline)). No source found what value the 2011 anime shows for this opening line [UNCLEAR]. The well-known **1.130205%** is the Beta line Okabe reaches much later, in VN Chapter 10 and anime episode 22 [CONFIRMED, single wiki] ([wiki.gg: Beta Attractor Field](https://scienceadventure.wiki.gg/wiki/Beta_Attractor_Field)).

The VN gives the morning to the minute. Okabe and Mayuri reach Radio Kaikan at **11:43 AM**, and a satellite-like object is sitting on the roof. Dr. Nakabachi's conference starts at **12:03**. At **12:26** Okabe receives an email containing only a static-filled video. A scream follows at **12:39**, and Okabe finds Kurisu apparently dead. At **12:54** he emails Daru that she has been stabbed [CONFIRMED for 12:39 by two wikis; other times single wiki] ([wiki.gg: VN plot](https://scienceadventure.wiki.gg/wiki/Steins%3BGate_(visual_novel)/Plot); [Fandom: Timeline](https://steins-gate.fandom.com/wiki/Timeline)). That email is the **first D-Mail**. Sent by accident through the PhoneWave, it shifts the world from **Beta 1.130426% to Alpha 0.571024%**, the line the story then spends its first half on (VN Prologue–Chapter 03, anime episodes 1–7) [CONFIRMED] ([Fandom: D-Mail](https://steins-gate.fandom.com/wiki/D-Mail); [wiki.gg: Alpha Attractor Field](https://scienceadventure.wiki.gg/wiki/Alpha_Attractor_Field)). On the Alpha line the conference is recorded as cancelled, and Kurisu is alive [CONFIRMED, single wiki for the cancellation] ([Fandom: Timeline](https://steins-gate.fandom.com/wiki/Timeline); [Wikipedia: VN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steins;Gate_(visual_novel))).

Sources disagree on when that first mail arrived. Fandom's D-Mail table says July 23, 2010. wiki.gg says Okabe's phone shows it received on July 24, five days early. ### Convergence in action: the date of Mayuri's death moves, the death does not

In episode 21, when Okabe tries to take a car's impact for her, she pushes him aside and dies instead [CONFIRMED] ([Wikipedia: episode list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Steins;Gate_episodes)). ## Conclusion

The cleanest way to understand Steins;Gate is to separate what each method sends. Information changes history, memories replay it, and bodies leave tiny marks, while attractor fields decide which changes count. Seen that way, the plot is one long proof of the rule. Okabe can't save Mayuri by repeating days, because leaping never leaves Alpha. He can't save Kurisu by force either, because Beta's convergence wins. He escapes only by changing what was hidden while leaving what he observed untouched. Steins;Gate 0 doesn't break this logic. It reveals that the instructions for that final move were written on the branch where Okabe gave up, which makes the sequel the source of the original's ending rather than an alternative to it [INTERPRETATION, built on the CONFIRMED facts above].

For a project built on these notes, the practical point is that the event-level canon is solid while the numbers are not uniformly so. Plot facts, the 1% boundary, the 36-byte and 48-hour limits, 0.571024%, 1.130205%, 1.129848% and 1.048596% all rest on multiple sources or explicit citations. Timestamps, machine names, invention years and several "why" questions rest on a single fan wiki, or on fans openly arguing. Anything showing a decimal value or a clock time should carry a "per fan wiki" note. The places where the writers themselves reportedly left things undefined, especially how messages choose their destination line, should be shown as deliberately open rather than quietly resolved [INTERPRETATION].
