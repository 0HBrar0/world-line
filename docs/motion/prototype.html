A site that feels wound up and running

Three ideas: a background built like real clockwork, small moving details where something happens in the story, and smooth changes between states. Fonts and sizes are stand-ins; watch the movement.

1. The clockwork behind every page

Drawn darker here so you can see it; on the site it stays faint behind the text.

AN UNOFFICIAL FAN CHRONICLEOne text message to the past can rewrite history.
  • Real wheels, not solid cogs. Thin rims, cut-out spokes and hubs, in three layers of grey so they overlap like a movement seen from the back.
  • Gears that mesh. Each wheel drives the next: neighbours touch, turn opposite ways, and small ones turn faster.
  • A clock that keeps time. Tall Roman numerals, shaped hands, and a red second hand that ticks once a second with an escape wheel stepping behind it.

2. Small moving details

Each one is tied to something that happens: a jump, a message, a new step. They play once when triggered; here they loop so you can watch.

3. The rules that keep it calm

  • Slow behind, quick in front. Background wheels take one to four minutes a turn. Buttons, cards and panels move in about a fifth of a second.
  • One event, one movement. Nothing loops in front of the text. A detail moves when its thing happens, then rests.
  • Red moves, ink stays. Animated accents are red: the strand, the second hand, the rule under a heading.
  • Off means off. With reduced motion on the device or in Settings, everything here holds still and pages change instantly.
  • The map stays light. The chart keeps its plain background so dragging stays smooth; only the strand and hops animate there.
